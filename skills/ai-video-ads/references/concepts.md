# Concepts and scripts

## How to come up with ideas

Start from what the audience already says or searches, not from the product's feature list. For each idea write:
the **hook line** (first 1–2 s, spoken and on screen), the **angle** (why this person is talking), the **audience**
(who stops scrolling), and the **CTA** (one action).

Two families, test both — they serve different jobs:

| Family | Feels like | Use when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Native / story** (a real moment: a family conversation, a call, a funny exchange) | not an ad until the end card | cold audiences, brand recall, scroll-stopping | must still be obviously about the product, or the spend is wasted on curiosity clicks |
| **Clear ad, UGC delivery** (a person to camera: explainer, list, question, testimonial) | an ad, but human | intent and conversion; people who already know the category | needs the category/product named in the first second |

A dialogue/roleplay ("someone calls an adviser and gets a calm answer") tends to convert because the viewer sees
someone like them ask the real question and get helped; the CTA is the natural next step of what they watched.
When one concept wins, make **variants of the same mechanic** (new caller, new doubt) before inventing new formats,
and test **hooks** on the winning body (same video, 3–4 new first 2 seconds) to learn *why* it won.

## Strong UGC formats (any category)

- **"What is X, in 15 seconds"** — expert to camera, 3 things on chips. For people new to the category.
- **"Have you ever reviewed your X?"** — someone who has had one for years. Targets existing customers.
- **"3 things to check before buying X" / "3 mistakes with X"** — list, counted on fingers; saves and shares.
- **"X for your parents / your kids"** — the buyer is not the beneficiary; matches a common search intent.
- **"How much does X cost?"** — answer honestly ("depends on…") and send to the comparison/quote.
- **Story-time** ("My mum asked me if I had X… I didn't even know what it was") — native but names the product.
- **Comment reply** — a comment card on screen, the expert answers it.

## Script rules

- 10 s ≈ 25–30 spoken words at a natural native pace; 15 s ≈ 40. Write to the model's clip length (check it; some
  models are fixed-length).
- Name the product category in the first sentence when the ad must be clear.
- Spoken URLs: write them as said ("brand punto com", "brand dot com"); the caption will show the real URL.
- One idea per video; one CTA. Dialogue lines short enough to cut between speakers.
- Write how natives talk (contractions, fillers, regional idiom); ask the owner to approve tone before generating.
- Run every line past the brief's claim rules. Hedge where the product varies ("depends on the policy").

## Show before spending

Present ideas as a short list with hook + one-line body + why it converts; let the owner pick. Then show the final
script and the stills; generate video only after a yes (or when the owner explicitly said "surprise me").
