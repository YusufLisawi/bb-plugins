# Series — writing Part 2 and beyond

Part 1 earns the audience; the sequels keep it. The channel's farm series measured 3.2 M → 1.6 M → 0.59 M → 0.49 M →
0.16 M views. Its first sequel was a strong stranger-in-the-barn story, and the later ones drifted into longer
first-aid lessons (94–118 s). Keep ours ≤ 75 s and keep each part a story with a turn, not a lesson.

## The series bible (`episodes/<series>/series.md`, one per series)

Create it with Part 1 and update it at every delivery. A sequel's agent reads it first.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Canon | place, time (DAY N, the clock), what the world knows so far |
| Cast | name · age · one-line nature · voice id · caption colour · rig look (first appearance: part, shot) |
| Rules and mistakes so far | numbered across the series: RULE #1 … #7, MISTAKE #1 … — never restart the count |
| Running counters | the supplies card's rows, the herd, the water, the infected count. The values carry over |
| Plants → payoffs | open plants (planted, not yet paid off) and closed ones, with the part each was paid in |
| Comment questions | each part's ending question and the majority answer (read the comments, or ask the owner) |

## The sequel beat sheet (≈ 70 s)

| # | Beat | Template | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recap-hook (0–5 s) | a list of 3–4 things the hero has, ending on the cliffhanger item: "Jake has water, food, a locked gate, and someone in his barn." | **Don't speak the series name or "Part N"** (owner). The recap IS the hook. Show it on Polaroid-style photos of the last part's frames, labelled PART 1 |
| 2 | Audience callback (optional) | "Most of you said, [answer]." | only when the last part ended on a question; show a comment bubble with a like count |
| 3 | The cliffhanger resolved | the first 10–15 s pay off the last reveal, with a fresh turn ("Wait! I'm not one of them!") | the new character gets a nameplate |
| 4 | Rule beats (2–3) | action → "Rule number [N]. [≤ 6 words]." or "Mistake number [N]." → the cause in one sentence → what the hero does | the count continues from the bible. Each rule gets a card, a diagram or a tip pill |
| 5 | Pay off an old plant | the dog that barks at everything finally matters | at least one per part |
| 6 | Plant the next | an unticked box, a hidden bandage, a key in a pocket | the camera shows it clearly once, and nobody comments |
| 7 | Escalation at night | a time stamp ("3:12 AM.") → the threat moves → a hard choice | red-light grade and silhouettes ([look.md](look.md) § 4) |
| 8 | Ending | a dilemma about a character: "Would you [go get her / tell Jake / let her stay / turn her in]?" | it seeds the next part's callback |

## Sequel line craft (what changes from Part 1)

- **More fragments and time stamps.** Procedures become 1–3-word beats ("Good light, six feet away. Hands. Neck.
  Ankles."), each beat with its own shot or checklist tick.
- **Dialogue grows** to 4–6 short lines across 2 voices. One of them is the line people quote ("I hope I'm worth
  12 cows.").
- **A rule can be learned from a mistake:** "Mistake number two. He turns on a lamp. With the grid down, his window
  is the only light for miles."
- **Every sequel still has one running question** (will they reach the barn before the 40 do?), and it's answered
  or flipped by the last line.

## Continuity checklist (before CP1)

- [ ] The same rigs, looks and caption colours for returning characters (from the bible).
- [ ] The counters and supplies card values follow the last part (water 73 gal → 64 gal on day 2).
- [ ] The rule / mistake numbers continue, and the cards use the same design.
- [ ] Places look the same (the farm plan, the barn, the house), so reuse the last part's set code.
- [ ] The time of day moves forward (DAY 1 · 7:24 PM → 3:12 AM → DAY 2 · 7:08 AM).
- [ ] The last part's question is answered in the first 10 s.

## Part 1 → Part 2 handoff

Part 1's end card already sells Part 2: PART 2 · [the teaser in red, ≤ 5 words] · FOLLOW TO SURVIVE DAY 1. Write
Part 2's recap line at the same time as Part 1's ending, so the teaser and the recap match.
