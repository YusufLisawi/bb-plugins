# The story engines

Six engines cover every niche. Each one is a beat sheet: what each line must do, roughly when, and what the
picture does under it. Pick ONE per film. The universal craft (connectors, punches, causes, numbers, endings) is in
[writing.md](writing.md), and the shot types named below are defined in [pacing-and-shots.md](pacing-and-shots.md).

| Engine | Length | Words | Shots | Mean shot | Narration share | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Survival rules | 60–72 s | 160–200 | 35–45 | 1.4–1.9 s | ≈ 85 % | A cliffhanger (P1) · B dilemma (sequels) |
| 2 · Problem → fix build | 60–75 s | 160–200 | 25–35 | 1.9–2.6 s | ≈ 75 % | "Congratulations! You just built…" |
| 3 · Scale ladder | 55–65 s | 50–120 | 1–15 (one journey) | stages 2–6 s | 30–55 % (sound-led) | C mind-blow + reality anchor |
| 4 · Zoom ladder | 55–65 s | 100–150 | 3–10 (one journey) | levels 2–4 s | ≈ 70 % | C mind-blow |
| 5 · POV time-skip | 60–75 s | 130–180 | 15–25 | 2.5–4 s, every shot moving | ≈ 65 % | D debate + loop · B dilemma |
| 6 · Experiment story | 60–72 s | 160–200 | 28–38 | 1.8–2.4 s | ≈ 85 % | A cliffhanger |

The owner's ≤ 75 s rule applies to every engine, even where the channel runs longer (its sequels are 93–118 s).

## 1 · Survival rules — the farm engine

**Use it for** disasters (blackout, flood, wildfire, tornado, earthquake, heatwave, blizzard), zombie or outbreak
series, personal safety (followed home, a stranger at the door), wilderness (lost, a bear, the desert), history
("if you woke up in 1347 London…") and money traps ("if you win the lottery, do not quit your job tomorrow").

**The engine in one sentence:** a hero is in an everyday place when the world breaks. The obvious move kills you,
the smart moves are real rules with real causes, each fix creates the next problem, and a false calm ends on a reveal.

### Beat sheet (65 s, 17 lines; the farm's timings)

| # | Beat | ≈ Time | Template | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 0–4 s | "If [event] starts while you're [place], do not [obvious instinct]." | the hero in the place, the alert arriving (phone, siren, radio) → OTS: the alert screen |
| 2 | Reframe | 4–8 | "Without knowing it, you're already in one of the [best / worst] places to survive." | the place wide, DAY 1 · time HUD, resource pins pop on their words |
| 3 | Open loop | 8–12 | "But only if you don't make one very stupid mistake." | map zoom-out (where we are) → the hero moving |
| 4 | The natural mistake | 12–16 | "[Hero]'s first instinct is to [obvious move] to [reason]." | the hero acting it out (truck, door, stairs) |
| 5 | Correction | 16–20 | "No, [why it kills you, one clause]." | NO. slam on "No," → a map of the danger; RULE #1 card |
| 6 | The clock | 20–24 | "Instead, [hero] has about [N minutes] before [system] [fails]." | a countdown pill starts and ticks; hands doing the first task |
| 7 | Priority 1 | 24–26 | "First, [resource]." (2–3 words) | the resource piling up, a counter card climbs |
| 8 | The hidden dependency | 26–30 | "[Source] runs on [X], and when [X] goes, so does [source]." | a cross-section diagram, process chips light in turn, then everything greys |
| 9 | Fix → new problem | 30–34 | "He has a [fix], but [using it] creates a new problem." | the hero uses the fix → a worried close-up |
| 10 | Rule as fact | 34–39 | "[Here], [the fix] can [danger] from [distance] away." | a map with rings or arrows → the threat reacting; RULE #2 card |
| 11 | Complication | 39–41 | "And [hero] doesn't [live alone / have …]." (punch) | the first dependent (animal, kid, neighbour) |
| 12 | Inventory | 41–46 | "[N] [things], [N] [things], [N] [thing], and a [thing] that [funny / dangerous habit]." | one shot per item, count chips stack; the last item acts |
| 13 | Stakes | 46–49 | "[Keeping X] is going to be almost impossible." | a tilted map with every problem marked → the danger-phrase slam |
| 14 | Montage | 49–54 | "So he [action], [action], and [action]." | one shot per action (3–5 cuts), closing on a supplies card |
| 15 | False calm | 54–57 | "On paper, he could last here for [time]." | warm light, the hero relaxes (mug, smile) |
| 16 | Turn | 57–59 | "There was just one problem." | the smile drops → the animal or kid senses it |
| 17 | Reveal | 59–62 | "[Someone / Something] was already [inside the X]." | shock close-up → OTS at the threat → eyes in the dark → end card |
| — | End card | 62–65 | PART 2 · [teaser, red] · FOLLOW TO SURVIVE DAY 1 | dark card, a glitch-in |

**Second-person variant** (the channel's high-rise): the hero is "you". The time stamps are spoken ("6:42 AM.",
"Noon.", "11 PM."), a companion arrives with a nameplate (RUTH · 81 · ACROSS THE HALL), and 3 numbered rules are
each spoken as "Rule number one. [Rule in ≤ 6 words]." plus a one-sentence cause. It ends on a dilemma about a door
or a key ("Would you open them?").

**Rule cards:** at most 2–3 per film, ≤ 6 words each, and each one paraphrases the spoken line ("STAY AWAY FROM
CROWDS", "NOISE CARRIES FARTHER THAN YOU THINK"). The numbering continues across the series ([series.md](series.md)).

**Pitfalls:** rules without causes (a lecture), a hero who only stands and listens (every line needs him DOING it),
a reveal that wasn't planted (the barn must be on screen before "inside the barn"), and a threat shown too early (the
first threat shot is a silhouette or an eye glow in fog, never a full monster).

## 2 · Problem → fix build

**Use it for** "How a [simple thing] becomes a [impressive thing]": engineering (a shelter, a bunker, a treehouse, a
bridge), the body ("how your body fixes a cut"), money systems ("how $5 a day becomes a house"), nature ("how a
beaver turns a stream into a lake").

**The engine:** start with the naive version, then break it in a vivid way, fix it with one real spec, and repeat
4–6 times. Test it against the real force, add one crazy true fact and a real case, and close with a cosy checklist.

### Beat sheet (≈ 70 s, 16–20 lines)

| Beat | Template | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "If you [do the naive version], you have a [goal]." (its captions carry the title: no separate top title, owner rule) | the naive version already happening at frame 0: a dad digging, the family jumping in the hole |
| Failure 1 | "But [specific failure, with a vivid simile]." ("…rips off your plywood lid like a frisbee.") | the failure in action, fast |
| Fix 1 + spec | "So you [specific fix]." → "[Threat] tops [N]. Yours is built for [N + margin]." | the cutaway set gains the new part; a spec card (big orange number + small label) |
| Failure 2 → Fix 2 | "But after [time], [failure] like a [simile]." → "So you [fix]." → "These are [name of the part]." | the cutaway again, one part more built |
| Failure 3 → Test | "But a normal [part] [fails]." → "So yours gets tested." → "[A rig] fires [N] at [N]." | a test-rig shot, the impact, a spec card |
| Failure 4 → Fix 4 | "But sealed in, you [new problem]." → "So you add [part]." → "[A gets in], [B] doesn't." (a contrast pair) | a detail insert of the part |
| The payoff | "Up there, [the force]. Down here, just [calm]." | a split frame: the storm above, the family below |
| Crazy part | "Crazy part, [weird true fact]." → "[a real case: place, a number]." → "[outcome in 3 words]." | a map card or a Polaroid of the case |
| Ending | "Add [item], [item], and [a kid who hates thunder]." → "Congratulations! You just built a [thing]." | the finished cutaway, warm light, the family inside |

**Anchor set:** one cutaway cross-section that you return to after each fix, each time a little more built. It's the
film's map. **Pitfalls:** fixes without a failure first (a boring spec sheet), numbers without a picture, and more
than 6 cycles.

## 3 · Scale ladder

**Use it for** repeated actions that explode: fold paper, double rice or a penny, save $1 a day, stack people, count
to a billion, or keep accelerating.

**The engine:** one question, then a running number that climbs through landmarks everyone knows, all in **one
continuous camera journey**. The narration is sparse: each spoken line is "[N] [unit], [landmark]." The final line
is a reality anchor that reframes the whole climb.

### Beat sheet (≈ 62 s, 8–14 lines, 50–120 words)

| Beat | Template | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "What if you [folded a sheet of paper in half] [103 times]?" (the number orange in the caption; no separate top title) | the object on a desk, the action already starting |
| The rule (text, or spoken) | "Every fold doubles its thickness." | the first 3–4 steps fast; the counter ticks |
| Human landmark | "[14] folds, it's as tall as you." | the stack beside a person |
| Built landmark | "[23], taller than the [Empire State Building]." | the camera pulls out over a city |
| Sky / space | "[30] folds, and it's in space." | clouds, the curve of the Earth |
| Moon → Sun → planets | "[42], it reaches the Moon." … | the line passes each body, with a label + leader line |
| Stars → galaxies | "[83] folds, wider than our whole galaxy." "[88], past Andromeda." | the Milky Way spiral, Andromeda |
| Final | "[103], taller than the observable universe." | the universe as a ball, the line still going |
| Reality anchor | "In real life, the record is [12]." | back to the desk (optional), the counter frozen |

**The HUD carries it:** a giant counter top-left (FOLDS **103**, THICKNESS **107 billion light-years**) whose unit
switches as it grows (mm → in → ft → miles → light-years), plus one small sans text line at the top per stage.
Landmarks get a thin leader line and a label: NAME, then a distance in small caps. **Sound leads:** a tick per
step, a whoosh on each landmark, and music higher than in story films.
**Flat version:** a powers-of-ten journey of nested flat scenes. Each stage is drawn at its own scale, the camera
zoom is exponential in time, and you cross-fade at each 10× handoff ([look.md](look.md) § 8).
**Pitfalls:** landmarks nobody knows, too many words, and a final number with no anchor. Humour beats help ("The desk
gives up."), and so does a real-world comparison ("Nine centuries of the world's rice.").

## 4 · Zoom ladder

**Use it for** zooming in until something everyday turns strange (fingers touching, a phone screen, a drop of pond
water, a grain of sand), or zooming out from your street to the universe.

### Beat sheet (≈ 62 s, 16–22 short lines)

| Beat | Template | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "What if you zoomed in until [your fingers touched]?" | two fingers nearly touching, the zoom already moving |
| False belief | "Looks like they're touching." → "They're not." | the gap appears |
| Levels (6–9) | "[Thing], [one surprising fact]." ("Dead skin cells, you shed thousands every minute.") | each level is a new flat scene, with a label + scale bar (×10, ×1,000 …) |
| The mechanism | 3 tiny lines: "Electrons are negative." "Negative pushes negative." "The gap stops here, [size]." | the push drawn as arrows and a glow |
| Reframe | "That [push] is what you feel as [touch]." → "You've never [touched anything] in your life." | pull back to the hand |
| Final punch | "Even now, you're [floating on your chair]." | the viewer's world, a 2nd-person twist |

**Pitfalls:** levels without a fact (a slideshow), or a final line that isn't about the viewer.

## 5 · POV time-skip

**Use it for** "What if every [phone / adult / car] stopped… except yours?", "What if you were the last kid on
Earth?", or history POV ("You wake up on the Titanic, April 14, 1912").

**The engine:** "you" are the camera. A sudden change, then a time-skip HUD jumps forward (+1 MIN → +60 YEARS). Each
jump shows one consequence, alternating world-scale and intimate moments, and it ends on a two-sided question.

### Beat sheet (≈ 70 s, 18–24 short lines)

| Beat | Template | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open (another voice, 1–2 lines) | a normal-life line from someone you love: "[Come on! We're gonna miss it!]" | the normal world, the viewer's hands in frame |
| The change | "[Every X on Earth] just [stopped / vanished]." → "Except [you / yours]." | the world changes in one beat; a big running number appears |
| Minutes | 2 fragments: the first strange consequence | phone UI, a counter, the viewer's hands |
| Hours → days | a world-scale consequence, then a darker turn ("Some aren't smiling.") | crowds, news tickers, the time-skip pill +1 HOUR → +1 DAY |
| Intimate beat | one personal line, often with a voicemail or call in another voice | phone close-up, soft light |
| Years | a jump that shows the long cost ("[The youngest child on Earth is 10.]" → "You're 85.") | the world aging, lights going out |
| End state | the final consequence in ≤ 8 words | the Earth from orbit, or an empty room |
| Debate | "[Option A] or [option B]. Who has it worse?" | a question card with COMMENT 👇 |
| Loop | "You'd give anything to go back." | back to the first frame |

The channel's version is "Every woman on Earth vanished… except you" (2.7 M). Its structure is above. Its content
went dark and adult, and ours must not. Premises that keep the structure and stay family-friendly: every phone on
Earth stops except yours · every adult vanishes and you're 12 · you're the only person who doesn't fall asleep for a
week · money disappears overnight.

**HUD:** a time-skip pill (WITHOUT [X] · **+1 DAY**) plus one running number that changes meaning over time (BORN
TODAY 7,500 → 0). **Pitfalls:** time jumps without a new picture, and an ending that isn't a real two-sided choice.

## 6 · Experiment story

**Use it for** "This guy did [odd thing] every day for a month": nature (sugar on an anthill, a trail camera), food,
money or habit experiments.

### Beat sheet (≈ 68 s, 18–22 lines)

| Beat | Template | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "This guy [dumps two pounds of sugar on an anthill] every day for a month." (captions carry it, orange keywords) | the action already happening |
| Expectation | "People said it would [rot the nest]." → "It didn't." | a quick "what people imagined" image, then a cut to the truth |
| Result | "By morning, [every grain was gone]." | the spot, a big stat card (2 LB · GONE IN ONE NIGHT) |
| The mechanism (3–5 steps) | "[Ants can't chew crystals.]" → "[A worker licks the sugar wet…]" → "[The first ant lays a scent trail home.]" | macro shots, one per step; labels on new parts (SOCIAL STOMACH) |
| Escalation | "So he went bigger, [44 pounds]." | the bigger dose, a stat card |
| The hidden world | "Because this [mound] is an iceberg." → "The [dome] is just the roof." + 2 numbers | a cutaway: tunnels, 3 FT UNDERGROUND, 300,000 ANTS INSIDE |
| Long-term | "And every year, [the mound comes back bigger]." | YEAR 1 → YEAR 3 cards |
| Cliffhanger | "But [sugar is just fuel]." → "[To grow babies, ants need protein.]" → "[And adult ants can't even swallow it.]" | close-up, PART 2 |

**Why the channel's version underperformed (65 K):** the reveal was a biology lesson, not a surprise, and the
experimenter had no face. **Fix:** give the experimenter a face and reactions, make the reveal change what we thought
was happening (stakes for the yard, the neighbour, the guy), and keep the cliffhanger a question the viewer wants
answered.

## A weak engine to avoid: the myth list

"These 3 pictures you saw in school are totally wrong" (93 s, 257 words, 26 K views): a talky list with promises
("the one that melts my brain") and no stakes. Turn the best item into a **scale ladder** (the Earth–Moon gap: every
planet fits in it) or a **zoom ladder** (the atom) instead.
