# Writing — how these stories are written

> [!IMPORTANT] Before line craft, make the story work: use the story-studio skill's `references/story-writing.md` (Writers Room engine: protagonist engine, therefore/but beats, world rules as pressure, plot-hole Q&A) and the series bible `episodes/_series/<series>/bible.md`, so canon never breaks across parts. The story doc must be approved before any voice or build.

The script is the film's skeleton. Every cut, caption, sound and graphic hangs on a spoken word, so a weak line can't
be saved by drawing. Write it first, lint it, then build.

What carries across every niche:

- **Stakes or wonder.** The winners have either a person in danger who must choose (farm, last woman) or a scale the
  mind can't hold (paper, fingers). The losers explain things without anyone at risk and without a reveal.
- **One question runs the whole film.** Will Jake make it? How tall does the paper get? Are the fingers touching? Every
  line moves that one question forward. Nothing is a side fact.
- **The picture carries.** When the visual is strong, the words get fewer. The 6.7 M paper video says 59 words in 62 s,
  and a giant counter does the rest.
- **The ending is built for comments:** a cliffhanger, a yes/no dilemma about a character, or a mind-blow you want to
  argue about. The weakest video ends on "Follow so you don't miss it."

Our look stays the farm's flat vector for every niche. The channel's science videos are 3-D renders, but only their
writing and pacing transfer here, not their look.

## 2 · The concept gate (score before you write a single line)

Score each premise 0–2 on six questions. Write it only at **≥ 9 / 12**, and **≥ 10** for a series opener.

| | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Could it happen to me / do I wonder about it?** | no | someday, maybe | tonight / I always wondered |
| **Everyday place or object** | a niche job or lab | known but distant | home, car, phone, school, money, the supermarket |
| **One running question** | a list of facts | a loose theme | one question every line advances |
| **Picture-able** | talking heads, abstract | a few strong images | every line has an obvious picture |
| **True and checkable** | made up | partly | every rule and number has a source |
| **A turn** | none | a mild surprise | a twist, a mind-blow or a dilemma people will argue about |

Farm Part 1 scores 12, the paper fold 11, and "3 school pictures" 6. Put all three of your premises and their scores
in `brief.md` and say why the winner won.

## 3 · Line 1 — the hook

- **≤ 16 words** (farm: 15). It holds the premise AND the tension in one sentence.
- **Frame 0 shows it literally**, already moving: the kitchen with the phone buzzing, the paper being folded, the
  crowd with one woman in it.
- It uses a place or object everyone knows, plus ONE of these three:
  - a **counter-intuitive order:** "If the zombie apocalypse starts while you're on a farm, **do not drive into town**."
  - an **impossible action:** "What if you folded a sheet of paper in half **103 times**?"
  - an **uncanny change:** "Every woman on Earth just vanished. **Except you.**" (2 lines, 7 words)
- It never warms up first. "Have you ever wondered…", "Today we're looking at…" and "This is crazy" all lose the viewer.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Floods are one of the most dangerous natural disasters." | "If your car is caught in a flood, do not open the door." |
| "Let's talk about compound interest." | "What if you saved one penny and doubled it every day?" |
| "Ants are amazing creatures." | "This guy dumps two pounds of sugar on an anthill every day for a month." |

## 4 · The spine: every line starts with a connector

The viewer always knows how the new line relates to the last one. That is what makes 17 lines feel like one fall
forward. Use the "But / So" rule: each beat is either a **complication** (But…) or a **consequence** (So…), never
"and then".

| Role | Connector | Farm line |
|---|---|---|
| reframe | Without knowing it, | "Without knowing it, you're already in one of the best places to survive." |
| stakes / open loop | But only if | "But only if you don't make one very stupid mistake." |
| the natural mistake | [Hero]'s first instinct | "Jake's first instinct is to jump in his truck and drive to Wichita to see what's going on." |
| correction | No, | "No, that's exactly where everyone is going." |
| clock | Instead, … about [N] | "Instead, Jake has about 30 minutes before the power grid starts failing." |
| priority | First, | "First, water." |
| cause → effect | and when … so does | "His well runs on electricity, and when the power goes out, so does the well." |
| the fix creates a problem | but | "He has a generator, but starting it creates a new problem." |
| rule as a fact | On a quiet farm, | "On a quiet farm, an engine can give away your position from hundreds of yards away." |
| complication | And [hero] doesn't | "And Jake doesn't live alone." |
| stakes stated | going to be almost impossible | "Keeping this farm quiet is going to be almost impossible." |
| action montage | So he … , … , and … | "So he locks the gate, brings the animals closer to the house, and starts counting everything he has." |
| false calm | On paper, | "On paper, he could last here for weeks." |
| turn | There was just one problem. | "There was just one problem." |
| reveal | Someone was already | "Someone was already inside the barn." |

Other engines use the same idea with their own connectors: **But… So you…** (build), **[N] [unit], [landmark].**
(ladders), **Now… / [time stamp].** (POV), **People said… It didn't. / So he went bigger.** (experiment). See
[engines.md](engines.md).

## 5 · Line craft

- **One idea per line**, 2–18 words, with a mean of 9–12 words.
- **Alternate long and short.** After one to four long lines (10–18 words) comes a punch of 2–7 words. Farm word
  counts: 15 · 13 · 10 · 18 · **7** · 12 · **2** · 15 · 11 · 16 · **5** · 14 · 10 · 18 · **8** · **5** · **6**. The
  punches carry the turns: "First, water." "And Jake doesn't live alone." "There was just one problem."
- **Cause in the same sentence.** Never state a rule without its why. "When the power goes out, so does the well."
- **Each fix creates the next problem.** That keeps the question open until the last line.
- **Lists of three or four, counting down, with a funny or dark last item:** "30 chickens, 12 cows, 1 horse, and a dog
  that barks at absolutely everything."
- **Three actions in one line make a montage:** "So he locks the gate, brings the animals closer to the house, and
  starts counting everything he has." That one line gets 5 cuts.
- **Fragments for procedures and tension** (sequels and POV): "Hands. Neck. Ankles." · "Key. Lock. Door." ·
  "Pressure. Ten minutes. Don't peek."
- **Time stamps change scenes without words:** "6:42 AM." · "Noon." · "That night," · "Dawn."
- **Present tense.** One named hero (a first name only, e.g. Jake, Maya, Sam), or "you" for POV. The hook and the
  rules speak to "you" even in a third-person story.
- **Small words.** Mostly 1–2 syllables, and at most one technical word per film, explained in the same line:
  "keratin, the protein in your nails."
- **Numbers:** round, small and concrete ("about 30 minutes", "hundreds of yards", "weeks"). At most one per line
  except an inventory line, and every spoken number appears on screen.
- **No filler or meta, ever:** "In this video", "Let's dive in", "Did you know", "Here's the thing", "Make sure to
  follow", "Like and subscribe", "Stay tuned", "Without further ado".

## 6 · Facts that feel like secrets

A rule has three parts: **the surprising rule**, **its physical cause**, and **what the hero does about it**. The
channel's best ones are things a normal person can use tonight:

- At night, cold air near the ground bends sound back down, so it carries even farther.
- A candle can be seen from over a mile away, so cover the windows and use red light only.
- Night vision takes 20–30 minutes, and the edge of your eye sees better in the dark.
- Electric locks unlock when the power fails (so nobody is trapped). Fire doors swing toward the exit, so from the
  stairs you must pull.
- Your water heater holds about 40 gallons. Plan on one gallon a day per person.
- Tetanus lives in soil and manure, not in rust.

Keep a **fact ledger** in `brief.md`, one row per claim:

| # | Claim as spoken | Check | Source | How it's shown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "His well runs on electricity" | true: a submersible well pump needs power | a USGS / extension-service page | cross-section, chips light in turn |

Prefer official sources: Ready.gov / FEMA, CDC, NWS / NOAA, Red Cross, NASA, USGS, a university or extension
service. Round numbers honestly ("about"). Mark a legend as a legend on screen.

> [!WARNING] Safety
> Never teach something dangerous. Medical tips follow official first-aid advice. Weapons stay off screen (a cut to
> black plus a sound). Crime-safety tips follow police / government guidance. Suspicion is labelled ("police believe…
> not proven").

## 7 · Plants and payoffs

Every film plants 1–3 details that pay off later: one inside this film, and one for the next part. A plant is cheap
(a word in a list, a row on a card, an unticked box), and its payoff feels inevitable.

| Planted (where) | Paid off |
|---|---|
| "a dog that barks at absolutely everything" (farm P1, the animal list) | P3: the bark leads 40 infected away from the barn |
| the generator's noise (P1, rule 2) | P2: the stranger "followed it" for miles |
| MEDICAL · LOW, the red row on the supplies card (P1) | P4: the nursing student, "Low becomes medical: Sadie." |
| the barn (P1, the porch shot looks at it) | the P1 cliffhanger and all of P2 |
| the unticked LEFT ARM on the bite checklist (P2) | P3: "Jake never checked the left arm. You did. It wasn't barbed wire." |

Keep a **plants → payoffs ledger** in `brief.md` (plant, line or shot, payoff, part). A sequel's first job is to pay
off at least one plant from an earlier part.

## 8 · Endings (pick one, and write it before the middle)

| Type | Shape | Example | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Cliffhanger** | false calm → "There was just one problem." → a past-perfect reveal; end card PART 2 + a teaser line | "Someone was already inside the barn." | survival P1, experiment |
| **B · Dilemma** | a yes/no question about a character's choice | "Would you go get her?" · "Would you tell Jake?" · "Would you open them?" | survival sequels, POV |
| **C · Mind-blow + reality anchor** | the biggest number, then the real-world fact that reframes it | "103, taller than the observable universe." → "In real life, the record is 12." · "You've never touched anything in your life." | ladders, zoom |
| **D · Debate + loop** | a two-sided question, then a line that loops to the opening | "Last woman or last man. Who has it worse?" → "You'd give anything to go back." | POV |

Rules:

- The last spoken line has ≤ 8 words.
- The question names a choice, not an opinion ("Would you…?", not "What do you think?").
- Never make the last spoken line "Follow for part 2". The end card may say "FOLLOW TO SURVIVE DAY 1" in small type.
- In a sequel, the next part opens by answering the question with the majority: "Most of you said, tell him."

## 9 · Dialogue (other voices)

- **≤ 6 cast lines per 60–75 s, each 2–8 words.** Each one shows who the person is or turns the plot: "I'm not one of
  them!" · "I hope I'm worth 12 cows." · "Hold the door, please?"
- On a character's first entrance, a **nameplate:** NAME · AGE · one fact ("SADIE · 20 · nursing student",
  "RUTH · 81 · across the hall").
- Each speaker gets their own caption colour (see [pacing-and-shots.md](pacing-and-shots.md) § 5).
  An off-screen or phone voice is italic.

## 11 · Before you voice it

- [ ] Line 1 ≤ 16 words, and frame 0 can show it.
- [ ] Every line starts with a connector or is a deliberate punch, and there's no "and then".
- [ ] A 2–7-word punch follows every 1–4 long lines (never 5 long in a row), and the mean is 9–12 words.
- [ ] Every rule has its cause, and every number is round and will be on screen.
- [ ] The fact ledger has a source for every claim. The plants ledger has ≥ 1 in-film payoff and ≥ 1 next-part plant.
- [ ] The ending type is chosen, and the last spoken line is ≤ 8 words.
- [ ] `lint_script.py` passes.
