# Story: angle, hook, structure, script

A film is sold in its first 1.5 seconds and remembered by one line. Decide the **concept** and the **angle**, write the **script**, and only then touch code.

## A proven arc (35–42 s at 30 fps)

Most of the finished films follow this arc, each dressed in a different shape (below). Treat it as a default, not a law: the concept decides the shape. The only fixed points are a hook in the first 1.5 s, a clear turn where the product arrives, and the brand ending plus CTA. Everything between them is yours to arrange.

| Act | When | Job | Example: *Every business runs on questions* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–2 s | Stop the scroll: a real person asking a real question, a contradiction, a timer, a poster | Four customers ask four businesses, in their own voices |
| **Problem** | 2–10 s | Make the pain concrete: people, hours, channels, languages, a number | "…day and night, every channel, every language." |
| **Turn / drop** | 10–15 s | The music drops on the product name; the mark draws itself | "Meet **Brainfast**" |
| **Proof** | 15–26 s | 2–4 product moments, each with a visible **result** (booked, tracked, captured, handed over) | One agent answers shop, clinic, estate agent, SaaS |
| **Reassurance** | 24–31 s | Answer the objection: *can I see it? can I fix it? can a human step in?* | "You see every conversation. Give feedback once…" |
| **Payoff / scale** | 30–33 s | It grows with you: the wall, counters, a team of agents | "…so it grows as fast as you do." |
| **Final hit** | ~33–35 s | "Brainfast. Create a new brain, fast." Lockup on the music's biggest hit | everything collapses into one point |
| **CTA** | last 5 s | "Build your first agent for free, at brainfast dot A I." Hold 1.5 s after the URL | pill + URL + tap |

Shorter (15–20 s): hook → drop → one proof → hit → CTA. For no-voice showreels, see the showreel in [films.md](films.md).

## Other shapes

The shape is how the arc is told. Pick the one that fits the concept, or combine two. A shape nobody has used yet is often the fastest way to make a film feel new.

| Shape | The spine | Suits | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metaphor world** | the whole film lives inside one image; the product UI appears *inside* that world | trust, onboarding, "what it is" | the glass box, the hire |
| **Time-lapse / a day** | one business across a night or a week; a clock or sky is the progress bar | always-on, scale | the Night Shift, no one waits |
| **One continuous shot** | a single camera travels through one world and never cuts | a flow with several parties (customer → agent → team) | when it doesn't know |
| **Before / after** | the same moment twice; the product is the wipe between them | pain-to-relief, owners | two shops |
| **Countdown / list** | "5 things it did while you slept"; a numbered beat per proof | punchy social cuts | untested |
| **Rapid fire** | a question-and-answer montage cut to the music's rhythm; the pace is the argument | breadth: industries, languages | untested |
| **Reverse reveal** | open on the result (a full calendar, a closed deal), then rewind to how it happened | outcomes, ROI without numbers | untested |
| **Live build** | the UI is the world: docs fly in, an agent is built, it answers, in real time | "how easy is it" | one link |
| **Quiz / game show** | the viewer plays along: A or B, a countdown, a reveal; rounds repeat the proof | curiosity, "sounds human", engagement | guess the AI |
| **Explainer episode** | teach one idea the viewer already wonders about; the product arrives only at the end or as one fair option | reach, trust, non-ad feeds | what is an agent, chatgpt vs claude |
| **Decision map** | a yes/no path through the options, fair to each; the last stop is you | comparisons (tools, DIY vs buy) | which AI tool |
| **Pure type** | kinetic typography carries the whole story, with product flashes as punctuation | manifestos, short teasers | the showreel |
| **Bad review yapping** | a 1-star review that is secretly glowing, read deadpan, then unpacked like a creator ("okay, let's unpack this"); the payoff re-rates it | humour, native feeds, any single benefit | 1-star reviews ep. 1–2 |
| **Case study** | one real customer, their setup, the workflow, then their real numbers | proof for one niche | the eye clinic |
| **Playbook** | how a niche would use it, step by step, with no claimed results | niches without a live customer yet | school admissions, internal helpdesk |

The universal rules still apply to every shape: one idea per beat, a result shown for every claim, the product's real strings, the brand ending.

## Angle catalogue

**Proven (each one shipped and was approved):**

1. **Every business runs on questions** *(universal, bright)*. Customer voices open it, a wall of questions, one agent answering many industries, then feedback that sticks.
2. **The glass box** *(trust, sleek dark)*. "Most AI is a black box." Watch every conversation, fix an answer once, and it learns for good.
3. **No one waits** *(human, warm)*. Four people in four cities at four hours, all waiting. Answered at once, in their language, and the timer rolls back to 0:00.
4. **The hire** *(playful, bold)*. A WANTED poster no human can fill. Brainfast applies, gets HIRED, is reviewed, is PROMOTED, and becomes employee of the month, every month.
5. **When it doesn't know** *(the escalation loop)*. The one question that matters gets lost in the chaos. The agent answers what it knows, asks the team what it doesn't, sends the reply back, and learns it.
6. **The night shift** *(story, night → morning)*. The owner closes up and the customers don't stop. The agent works the night, and the owner wakes up to results.
7. **Showreel** *(15 s, no voice, horizontal)*. A spark, a pen-drawn mark, the kinetic tagline, feature flashes, the lockup.

**Fresh angles to try next** (untested; pick one when the user asks for something new):
- **Before / after:** a split screen. Left is the owner drowning at 11 PM; right is the same moment with Brainfast.
- **Ask it anything:** a rapid-fire montage of ten questions from ten industries, each answered in under a second.
- **One agent, ten languages:** the same question rolls through ten languages and each answer lands in kind.
- **From docs to agent:** your PDFs, site and FAQs fly in, and a working agent comes out in 60 seconds.
- **The owner's phone:** the notification storm goes quiet, day by day, as the agent takes over.
- **Your best employee, cloned:** the best rep's answers become the agent's, and now they're everywhere at once.

## Hooks that work

- **A real voice:** a customer VO cameo asking the question, with the card typing each word as it's spoken. See [voice.md](voice.md) § Cast lines.
- **A contradiction:** "Most AI is a black box." Then the black cube turns to glass.
- **A clock:** "11:47 PM", or a `WAITING 0:04` timer counting up next to an unanswered message.
- **A format play:** a WANTED poster, an unread badge racing to 99+, a notification avalanche.
- **The lost thing:** one high-value request (a $4K catering order) sinking under the flood.

## Script rules

- **Length:** 95–110 words, which is 35–42 s at 2.8–3.0 words/s after the edit ([voice.md](voice.md)).
- **Rhythm:** one idea per sentence, 3–9 words each. Use lists of three ("It books. It sells. It captures every lead."), then one longer line to breathe.
- **The drop word:** the product name, said on its own at the turn: "Meet Brainfast," / "Brainfast is a glass box." / "Then Brainfast applied." The music drop is placed on this word.
- **The ending, always:** `[confident] Brainfast. Create a new brain, fast. [friendly] Build your first agent for free, at brainfast dot A I.` (or `Try it for free, at brainfast dot A I.`). Write "brainfast dot A I": v3 reads it right and speech-to-text returns "brainfast.ai".
- **Expression tags:** use one per sentence and follow the emotional arc: `[soft] [curious] [tense] [wry] [intrigued]` for the problem, `[confident] [excited]` for the turn, `[upbeat] [energetic] [impressed]` for the proof, `[reassuring] [warm] [satisfied]` for control, `[inspired] [building]` for scale, `[confident] [friendly]` for the end. The tags are not spoken; `vo_take.py` verifies that.
- **Headlines condense, they don't transcribe.** "It answers like your best people" becomes **Answers.** "Give feedback once and it learns for good" becomes **Feedback once. Learned for good.** Each headline word still rises on the frame it's spoken.
- **Never:** prices or plan names, competitor names, medical or legal advice, or promises the product can't keep. Bookings need a connected calendar and order status needs a connected store: show them, but flag them in the README.
- **Universality:** use 4–5 businesses from different industries (store, clinic, estate agent, hotel, SaaS, agency, school, salon). Use visibly different people, and include one line in another language (Spanish lands well).

## One customer as a through-line

Films feel written, not assembled, when one person travels the whole story. In *When it doesn't know*, Elena's catering request is the lost question in the chaos. After the drop it's answered instantly, becomes a captured lead, escalates, gets the team's reply, and becomes learned knowledge. Plant the person in the hook and pay them off in the proof.

## The brief (fill in before any code)

`films/<slug>/brief.md` (from `film-template/`) has three sections:

1. **Concept:** what you studied, the idea in one sentence, the world, the signature move, the motion language, the look, the sound world, and how it differs from the closest existing film.
2. **Message:** angle, audience, hook (the first image and first words), problem, turn, proof moments (real UI strings and results), reassurance, payoff line, CTA, formats, voice and length.
3. **Beat map,** after the voice exists: each beat, the word it lands on, the picture, and the sound.
